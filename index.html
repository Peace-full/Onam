the legend

The benevolent king who returns home once a year.

Kerala once belonged to Mahabali a king so just that nobody went hungry, no one lied, and every door remained open.

Growing jealous of his reign, the gods sent a young boy named Vamana to humble him. Vamana asked for just one thing: "three paces of land." The generous Mahabali smiled and agreed.

Vamana's first two steps miraculously covered the earth and the sky. With no land left for the third step, the noble king bowed and offered his own head. Touched by his devotion, Vamana granted him one final boon: to visit his beloved people every year.

That beautiful homecoming is Onam.

His arrival is celebrated on Thiruvonam the grandest day of the ten.

The Flower Carpet

A vibrant carpet of fresh flowers, laid out to say welcome.

The Pookalam is the first thing King Mahabali sees upon his return—a breathtaking floral pattern laid fresh at the doorstep every morning.

Beginning as a small, simple design on Atham, it gains one new ring every day, blooming into a magnificent masterpiece by Thiruvonam. Only the freshest, most colorful petals are chosen for this beautiful art.

The Grand Feast

26 dishes.
One banana leaf.

The Onasadhya is the crown jewel of the festival—a majestic spread of warm rice and up to twenty-six traditional dishes, served entirely on a fresh banana leaf.

Traditionally savored by hand to fully experience the authentic flavors and textures, the meal always concludes with Payasam—the sweetest, most comforting dessert on earth.

Eat like a king. It is exactly what King Mahabali would want for his people.

Quick Facts

Onam in four lines.

01

It is the premier harvest festival of Kerala—falling in Chingam, the first month of the Malayalam calendar.

02

Joyously celebrated by millions of Malayalis across the globe, uniting communities every single year.

03

A Pookalam uses only fresh flowers—with one new vibrant ring added each morning across the ten days.

04

New clothes, traditional games, and a spirit of giving back—Onam is truly a festival of pure joy.
